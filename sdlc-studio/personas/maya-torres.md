## Who They Are

Maya is 20 and a junior in computer science at a state university. She has ADHD. She knows exactly what she should
do; starting is the wall. This term she takes ENVS 119, CS 146, POLS 15, PHIL 134 and CS 47, and works 12 hours a week
at the campus help desk. Her Canvas shows everything at once, and every item looks equally urgent. Zeno is "for brains
that know what to do but can't start". That is Maya.

## End Goals

*Most important first. The design is judged against these.*

1. Start the work that matters each day within minutes, not hours.
2. Hand in every assignment on time this term, without all-nighters.
3. Always know the one next thing, without keeping lists or planners.
4. Feel less behind and less stressed about school.

## Experience Goals

- Calm, not overwhelmed: one thing at a time.
- Never judged or nagged. A slip is not a failure.
- Quick and satisfying, short like a reel.
- Private: her health and her life stay hers.

## Behaviours & Context

- **Environment:** her laptop in the library (sound off) and in her room; her phone everywhere. She works in bursts
  between classes and her shifts.
- **Frequency:** opens Zeno a few times a day. Without help, most of her work happens the night before it is due.
- **Proficiency:** fluent with apps and AI chat. She tried Notion, Todoist and a paper planner and dropped each within
  a week.

## Frustrations

- Canvas, her inbox and her calendar each show everything. She cannot tell what matters first.
- "I know what to do, I just can't start." The blank page wins for an hour.
- To-do apps become one more thing to manage, and the long list makes her feel worse.
- AI chat will write the essay, but then it is not hers, and it does not help her start the next one.
- Timers that beep and apps that ping make her anxious, so she mutes them and then misses things.

## Scenario: a day with Zeno

**Tuesday, 8:35 AM.** Maya has 25 minutes before CS 146. She opens Zeno and the card is already there: "Outline claim
2 · 15 min · PHIL 134 paper · due Fri". She taps Start. The stopwatch counts up; lo-fi plays. At minute 11 a soft line
appears on the card: "Still with the outline? Keep going." She does not answer, and nothing else happens. At 14
minutes she taps Done: the DONE stamp, then "Nice. Next: Find two quotes for claim 2, 10 min." She taps "That's
enough today" and goes to class. Zeno saves her place.

**12:15 PM.** Between classes she opens Zeno and taps Start on the quotes. She drifts to her phone for eight minutes.
Zeno does not see her phone and does not care. When she comes back to the tab, the card reads "Back. 13 min on 'Find
two quotes for claim 2'." She finishes and taps Done. That is two steps today, and her streak holds.

**4:00 PM.** She taps Talk: "I have the CS 146 exam next Thursday and… I haven't started." Zeno waits through her
pause, then answers: "Exam Thursday. Four one-hour study sessions, the first tomorrow at 4. Put them on your
calendar?" One tap books them.

**Later, as Zeno grows:** the sessions land on her main calendar in Zeno's color, titled "[Suggested] CS 146 exam
study", with no reminders. On Sunday evening Zeno offers one line: "3 goals moving, 1 slipping: POLS 15 reading. Plan
the week?" One tap drafts her week.
